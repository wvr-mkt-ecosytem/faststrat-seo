Across thousands of Google Ads accounts, [WordStream measured](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2014/03/17/what-is-a-good-conversion-rate) an average landing page conversion rate of 2.35%, with the top 25% at 5.31% or higher and the top 10% at 11.45% or higher. The gap isn't design budget, it's execution on a specific set of elements that most pages quietly skip.

**A landing page checklist is a systematic review of every element, from headline to hosting, that determines whether a visitor acts or bounces.** High-converting pages don't happen by intuition; they happen because someone verified each layer, from message-to-market match down to button contrast ratios. This checklist covers all 47 elements, organized by the order you should tackle them.

---

## 1. Strategy & Research: The 8 Foundations You Can't Skip (Items 1–8)

No amount of great copy saves a landing page built on unclear strategy. These eight items come first because they define every decision that follows.

**Item 1, Single conversion goal.** Every landing page must do one thing. If you're asking visitors to book a demo *and* download a PDF *and* follow you on LinkedIn, your conversion rate will reflect that confusion. Define one primary action before you write a word.

**Item 2, Documented audience persona.** Not a generic "marketing manager, 35–45." Go deeper: what's their biggest current frustration? What objection will they raise? What have they already tried and abandoned?

**Item 3, Traffic source alignment.** A visitor from a cold Facebook ad needs more context than someone clicking a branded search ad. Your page's awareness level must match where the traffic is coming from, message mismatch kills more pages than bad design.

**Item 4, Competitor page audit.** Review 3–5 competitor pages. Note their headline angle, offer structure, and guarantees. Your job isn't to copy, it's to find the positioning gap they're leaving open.

**Item 5, Unique Value Proposition (UVP) defined.** Can you complete this sentence in one breath: *"We help [specific audience] achieve [specific outcome] without [the thing they dread]"*? If not, the page won't convert.

**Item 6, Funnel stage awareness.** A TOFU (top-of-funnel) page for cold traffic needs education. A BOFU retargeting page needs proof and urgency. These are structurally different pages with different copy priorities.

**Item 7, Mobile-first design decision.** Mobile accounted for [52.57% of worldwide web traffic in July 2026, per StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/platform-market-share/desktop-mobile-tablet). Check your own analytics for your market, but if you're designing desktop-first and adapting down, you're building the wrong direction.

**Item 8, Analytics tracking confirmed before launch.** GA4 conversion events, Meta Pixel, LinkedIn Insight Tag, set it up before you go live, not after. Retroactive data gaps are unrecoverable.

## 2. Above the Fold: Pass the 8-Second Test (Items 9–16)

Visitors decide whether to stay or leave in under 8 seconds. Everything above the fold must immediately answer: *What is this? Why should I care? What do I do next?*

**Item 9, Headline answers "what's in it for me?" in ≤10 words.** Specificity converts. "Double your email open rate in 14 days" beats "Take your email marketing to the next level" every time.

**Item 10, Subheadline adds proof or specificity.** If your headline makes a claim, the subheadline supports it with a mechanism or credential. Use a template like *"Used by [number] SMBs across Mexico, Colombia, and the US"*, filled in with a number you can actually verify.

**Item 11, Hero visual shows the outcome, not the product.** A photo of a relieved business owner reviewing growing revenue stats converts better than a screenshot of your software dashboard.

**Item 12, Primary CTA visible without scrolling.** On both desktop and mobile. No exceptions.

**Item 13, No navigation menu.** Remove the header nav entirely. Every link you add is an escape route. In [HubSpot's own A/B tests](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/landing-page-navigation-ht), removing navigation produced a 0–4% bump on top-of-funnel pages and lifts of 16% and 28% on middle-of-funnel pages.

**Item 14, Trust signal visible above the fold.** A recognizable client logo, a "Featured in" badge, or a star-rating snippet. Something that signals *other people have already trusted this.*

**Item 15, Mobile load time under 2.5 seconds.** Test with Google PageSpeed Insights. Google's data shows mobile conversions drop measurably beyond the 2-second mark, every additional second compounds the damage.

**Item 16, Above-fold A/B variant planned.** Before launch, write your first test hypothesis. Start with headline or CTA copy, these consistently produce the largest measurable differences.

## 3. Copy & Messaging: The Conversion Layer (Items 17–24)

Copy is where most landing pages either earn trust or quietly lose it. These checklist items are about discipline, removing everything that doesn't serve the visitor's decision.

**Item 17, Copy addresses pain, not features.** "Stop losing customers to slow follow-up" speaks to a SMB owner. "Automated CRM with workflow triggers" doesn't, at least not as a first impression.

**Item 18, Bullets are ≤7 and outcome-focused.** Long bullet lists signal you don't know what matters most to the reader. Each bullet should describe a result: *"Save 6 hours/week on social media scheduling"* vs. *"Social media management tool."*

**Item 19, Benefit-to-feature ratio is at least 3:1.** For every feature you mention, describe three things it does for the reader. Most pages invert this ratio, and their conversion rates show it.

**Item 20, CTA copy uses power words and specificity.** *"Get My Free 30-Minute Growth Audit"* converts better than *"Submit"* or *"Get Started."* The reader should know exactly what happens when they click.

**Item 21, Objections are addressed in the copy.** Think through the top 3 reasons someone wouldn't convert. Too expensive? Address ROI. Unsure if it applies to them? Add industry-specific proof. Short on time? Reduce perceived effort in the copy.

**Item 22, Urgency or scarcity is real, not manufactured.** Fake countdown timers that reset destroy trust the moment visitors notice. Real scarcity, limited spots, a genuine offer deadline, works because it's honest.

**Item 23, Copy reads at a grade 7–8 level.** Use Hemingway App or Readable.com. Short sentences, active voice, no jargon unless your audience specifically uses it themselves.

**Item 24, Tone matches the ad or email that drove traffic.** If your Facebook ad was conversational and warm, and your page reads like a corporate brochure, you've broken the visitor's psychological continuity. This is one of the most overlooked conversion killers.

## 4. Trust Signals & Social Proof (Items 25–31)

Visitors don't know you. Every trust signal is a shortcut that says: *others have already taken this risk, and it worked out.*

**Item 25, Testimonials include full name, photo, and company.** *"J.M., Business Owner"* means nothing. *"Jorge Morales, CEO, Distribuidora del Valle, Monterrey"* means something verifiable.

**Item 26, Testimonials contain specific results.** A testimonial of the form *"We increased qualified leads by 34% in 60 days"*, for example, is far more persuasive than *"Great service, highly recommend!"* Always ask clients for their own real numbers.

**Item 27, Logo bar features recognizable brands or local anchor clients.** Even one recognized regional brand lifts credibility significantly for SMB audiences who operate in the same market.

**Item 28, Review counts and ratings are visible near the CTA.** An aggregate rating like *"4.8/5 from 200+ reviews on Google"* placed near your call-to-action adds third-party validation exactly where decisions are made.

**Item 29, Security badges appear near form fields.** An SSL lock icon or a plain *"Your data is safe, we never share it"* line reduces form abandonment by lowering the perceived risk of submission.

**Item 30, Risk-reversal statement is explicit.** A money-back guarantee, free trial, or "cancel anytime" clause directly reduces the perceived cost of saying yes. Spell it out in plain language near the CTA.

**Item 31, Privacy policy link is visible near email fields.** It's a regulatory requirement in many markets and, more practically, a visible trust signal. Its absence makes privacy-conscious visitors pause.

## 5. Form & CTA Optimization (Items 32–38)

The form is the finish line. Most pages lose the race right here.

**Item 32, Form asks for the minimum possible fields.** Every additional field drops completion rates. For lead gen, name + email is usually enough. Add phone only if your sales process genuinely requires it upfront.

**Item 33, CTA button color contrasts clearly with the page background.** Not just "bright", contrast. Run it through a WCAG AA contrast checker (4.5:1 ratio minimum). Invisible buttons don't get clicked.

**Item 34, CTA copy is action-specific and written in the first person.** *"Start My Free Trial"* outperforms *"Start Free Trial."* Small linguistic shift, measurable conversion difference across split tests.

**Item 35, Multi-step form used for complex offers.** If you genuinely need 6+ fields (a custom quote, a detailed intake), break it into 2–3 steps. Completion rates improve because commitment builds progressively, people don't abandon halfway through.

**Item 36, Inline field validation shows errors in real time.** Don't wait until form submission to flag an invalid email. Inline validation reduces frustration and abandonment at a critical moment.

**Item 37, Thank-you page defines the next step.** *"Thanks, we'll be in touch!"* is wasted real estate. Confirm what happens next (*"Your audit report arrives in 15 minutes"*), offer a related resource, or invite a share. The post-conversion moment is underrated.

**Item 38, Confirmation email triggers within 60 seconds.** Speed signals professionalism. An immediate confirmation also sets expectations and eliminates the *"did it actually work?"* anxiety that drives re-submissions or drop-off.

## 6. Technical & Speed Essentials (Items 39–43)

These elements don't show up in your design, but Google and visitors both feel them.

**Item 39, Core Web Vitals are passing.** Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) under 2.5s, Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) under 0.1, Interaction to Next Paint (INP) under 200ms. Check via Google Search Console or PageSpeed Insights.

**Item 40, SSL certificate is active.** Any browser "Not Secure" warning kills conversions before the page even loads fully.

**Item 41, Mobile viewport is correctly configured.** The `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">` tag must be present and functional. Test on real devices, browser dev tools lie more often than you'd expect.

**Item 42, Schema markup is implemented.** FAQPage schema for pages with FAQ sections; Product or Service schema where applicable. This feeds Google's rich results and helps AI tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity understand and surface your content accurately.

**Item 43, UTM parameters are tracked through to conversion.** Your analytics should tell you not just *"12 leads this week"* but *"12 leads from campaign Y, ad Z."* Without UTM discipline, you can't optimize spend, you're just hoping.

---

## 7. Post-Launch: The Optimization Loop (Items 44–47)

A landing page isn't finished at launch. It's a hypothesis you test and improve on a schedule.

**Item 44, Heatmap tool is installed.** Hotjar (free up to 35 sessions/day), Microsoft Clarity (free, unlimited), or FullStory. Watch where people click, scroll, and abandon. Behavioral data replaces guessing.

**Item 45, A/B test hypothesis is written down before starting.** *"We believe changing the headline from X to Y will increase CTA clicks because Z"*, documented, with a specific metric to measure. Undocumented tests produce undocumented learnings.

**Item 46, Statistical significance threshold is set at ≥95%.** That is the conventional threshold testing tools use, [VWO among them](https://vwo.com/glossary/statistical-significance/). Don't call a winner after 50 conversions. Most SMB pages need 200–500 conversions per variant to reach significance. Tools like VWO, Google Optimize, or Convert calculate this automatically.

**Item 47, Monthly conversion review is on the calendar.** Recurring. Non-negotiable. Review conversion rate, heatmap data, form abandonment rate, and top exit points. The cadence matters as much as the review itself.

## FAQ: Landing Page Checklist

**How many elements does a landing page need to convert well?**
There's no magic number, but the above-the-fold section (items 9–16) and your form/CTA block (items 32–38) deliver the highest immediate impact. A page with 20 elements executed well will outperform a page with 47 done carelessly. Prioritize ruthlessly.

**What's a realistic conversion rate for a landing page in 2026?**
[WordStream's benchmark](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2014/03/17/what-is-a-good-conversion-rate) puts the average landing page at 2.35%, the top 25% at 5.31% or higher, and the top 10% at 11.45% or higher. SaaS trial pages and free-offer lead gen pages tend toward the high end; service businesses with longer sales cycles typically convert lower but with higher deal values per lead.

**Should a landing page have a navigation menu?**
Almost never. Navigation menus create decision paralysis and give visitors an easy exit before they've given the page a real chance. The only exception is a multi-offer hub where related options are genuinely complementary to the same visitor, even then, minimize it.

**How often should you update a landing page?**
Run at least one A/B test per month once you have sufficient traffic (aim for 500+ visitors/month before testing). Beyond that, review the full page quarterly for outdated testimonials, changed pricing, or expired offers, stale social proof actively harms trust.
