# How Much Does a Landing Page Cost in 2026?

A landing page in 2026 costs [$10 to $8,000](https://www.apexure.com/blog/landing-page-design-cost-break-down), depending on who builds it. DIY AI tools run $10–$99/month, freelancers charge $500–$3,000 per page, and agencies start around $2,000.

That range is accurate, but it doesn't answer the question that actually matters: which option is cheapest for *you*, once you count what your own time is worth? Most guides stop at the tool fee or the service quote. This one runs the full calculation.

## the real cost, at a glance

Three paths to a live landing page. Here's what each one costs, including the time you spend:

| Option | Tool or service fee | Your time | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY, Framer or Webflow | $10–$15/month | 8–20 hours | Building 4+ pages/year |
| DIY, Unbounce or Leadpages | $74–$99/month | 10–15 hours | Need A/B testing and conversion data |
| Freelancer | $500–$3,000, one-off | 2–4 hours | One page; time is your main constraint |
| Agency | $2,000–$8,000+ | 5–10 hours | High-stakes campaigns; need strategy, copy, and build in one |

The column that shifts the math is "your time." Say your time is worth $75/hour: eight DIY build hours cost $600 in real terms before you've paid for the tool. That narrows the gap between building yourself and hiring faster than most price guides suggest.

## DIY AI builders: what you actually pay

As of September 2026, the major builders charge the following (prices confirmed from each platform's pricing page):

- **Framer Basic**, $10/month billed annually ([framer.com/pricing](https://www.framer.com/pricing)): one custom domain, 50 GB bandwidth, built-in SEO, 1,000 AI credits/month
- **Webflow Basic**, $15/month billed annually ([webflow.com/pricing](https://webflow.com/pricing)): 300 static pages, custom domain, unlimited form submissions
- **Carrd Pro Standard**, $19/year ([carrd.co](https://carrd.co)): 10 sites, custom domain, basic forms
- **Unbounce Build**, $99/month or $74/month on annual billing ([unbounce.com/pricing](https://unbounce.com/pricing/)): unlimited pages, AI copywriting, 1,000+ integrations, up to 20,000 visitors/month
- **Leadpages Grow**, $99/month, 20% off on annual billing ([leadpages.com/pricing](https://www.leadpages.com/pricing)): unlimited pages, manual A/B testing, no visitor cap

Framer, Webflow, and Carrd cover a clean, fast-loading page built from a template. Unbounce and Leadpages add A/B testing and conversion analytics, which earn their higher monthly fee once you're running consistent paid traffic. Note that Unbounce A/B testing only starts at the [Experiment plan ($112/month annual)](https://unbounce.com/pricing/); on the Build plan you get the builder without the testing layer.

The cost most calculators omit is build time. [LinearDesign's data on landing page timelines](https://lineardesign.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-design-a-landing-page/) puts a simple page at 8 hours to two days for a non-specialist. Templates cut build time by roughly 40–60%, so a first-time builder working from a template should realistically count on 8–12 hours for a page they'd be comfortable sending paid traffic to.

At $75/hour of owner time, 10 build hours cost $750 in opportunity. Add $10 for Framer Basic and the real cost of one page is $760. Add $99 for Unbounce Build and it's $849, before copywriting, before revisions, before A/B testing.

If you build four or more pages per year, DIY tools are clearly the better choice because the monthly fee amortizes quickly across iterations. For a single page, the math is closer to hiring than it first appears.

## hiring a freelancer

Freelancer rates for a single landing page run [$500–$3,000](https://lineardesign.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-design-a-landing-page/). At the low end: a template-based build with your assets and your copy, delivered in three to five days. At the high end: custom design, conversion copywriting, mobile QA, and two revision rounds.

Most Upwork landing page designers bill $25–$75/hour. A 15-hour project at $50/hour is $750. Add one revision cycle and you're in the $875–$1,100 range for a competent mid-tier designer, without copywriting included.

Three things a freelancer delivers that a builder tool won't:

- Layout and hierarchy decisions, where the eye lands first, second, and where it leaves the page
- Speed from repetition: a specialist who has built 50 landing pages works faster than a first-timer learning an unfamiliar tool
- A clean handoff: one deliverable, no monthly subscription to manage after the project ends

**Copy is almost never included in a design quote.** A conversion copywriter for one landing page runs $300–$800 separately, and that's the line item most SMBs skip. [Unbounce's 2024 Conversion Benchmark Report](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/), drawn from 41,000 landing pages and 464 million visitors, found that pages written at a 5th–7th grade reading level convert at 11.1%, compared to 5.3% for professional-level writing. Copy clarity moves conversion rates more than design quality. Know that before you decide what to outsource.

Before you post a brief: specify the number of sections, which devices need testing, which integrations must be wired, and how many revision rounds are included. Without those terms in writing, scope expands and cost follows.

## agencies: when the cost is justified

An agency landing page project runs [$2,000–$8,000](https://www.apexure.com/blog/landing-page-design-cost-break-down) for a custom, conversion-focused build. Complex builds with dynamic content, CRM integrations, or multilingual support go higher.

The fee covers the full process: strategy, copy, design, development, QA, and revision rounds. What you're paying for beyond execution is coordination and accountability, a single contract across every discipline instead of you managing each one separately.

**The math that justifies the price:** Unbounce's [2024 benchmark data](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/) puts the median conversion rate at 6.6% across all industries. Say a page converts at 4% on 10,000 monthly visitors: moving to 7% means 300 additional conversions per month. At a $50 average order value, that's $15,000 in additional monthly revenue from one better page. At those numbers, a $5,000 agency build pays back in the first weeks of the campaign, not months.

**Where the agency rate doesn't hold up:** a four-week local promotion, a campaign with fewer than [1,000 monthly visitors](https://spotler.com/blog/how-many-visitors-do-you-need-to-run-a-valid-a-b-test), too few for statistically valid A/B tests, or any product where customer lifetime value can't absorb a $2,000+ upfront cost. Agency overhead is built into every project. For a page that runs one month and retires, that overhead earns nothing back.

## the break-even calculation most guides skip

DIY is cheaper than a freelancer only when your build time multiplied by your hourly rate comes in below the freelancer's quote. For most SMB owners, that break-even sits at around 10 hours of build time, which is close to what the first real build actually takes.

Here's the full math at $75/hour owner time:

| Scenario | Money out of pocket | Your time cost | Real total |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY on Framer, 10 hours | $10 | $750 | $760 |
| DIY on Unbounce Build, 12 hours | $99 | $900 | $999 |
| Freelancer at $700, 2 hours oversight | $700 | $150 | $850 |
| Freelancer at $1,200, 3 hours oversight | $1,200 | $225 | $1,425 |

At $75/hour, building on Framer ($760 real cost) beats hiring a $700 freelancer ($850) by $90. Add two hours of trial-and-error or one revision cycle to the DIY build, and the freelancer comes out ahead.

**The rule:** if a freelancer quotes less than (your hourly rate × expected build hours) + tool cost, hire the freelancer. If not, build it yourself and put the savings into ad spend.

At $50/hour owner time, a 10-hour Framer build costs $510 in real terms. Any freelancer under $510 saves you money. Most competent freelancers charge more than that.

**Where DIY wins clearly:** four or more landing pages per year. At $10–$74/month on annual billing, each additional page costs almost nothing incremental. At agency rates, each new variant is $2,000+. The tool advantage compounds with volume.

## which option fits your situation

**One campaign, one page, limited time:** hire a freelancer at $700–$1,200. Write a tight brief, fix deliverables in writing before work starts. If budget is tight, skip the copywriting scope and write the first draft yourself, test and refine once you have real conversion data.

**Consistent paid traffic, need A/B data:** [Unbounce Experiment at $112/month](https://unbounce.com/pricing/) on annual billing, or Leadpages Grow at $99/month ([leadpages.com/pricing](https://www.leadpages.com/pricing)). Both tools cap monthly visitors, so confirm your traffic volume fits before committing to annual billing. Leadpages has no visitor cap; Unbounce caps at [30,000/month on the Experiment plan](https://unbounce.com/pricing/).

**Single clean page for a pitch, waitlist, or low-traffic campaign:** Framer Basic ($10/month) or Webflow Basic ($15/month). Both produce fast, professional pages from templates. No built-in conversion analytics, which is fine for pages that don't receive paid traffic.

**Product launch with real ad spend:** agency at $3,000–$6,000 for a page you'll run for six to twelve months. The only context where full-process work reliably earns its cost.

**Testing a new offer before committing budget:** Carrd Pro Standard at $19/year. Build the page in an afternoon, confirm the offer gets traction, then upgrade the tool and the design once you know the concept converts.

## FAQ

### How much does a basic landing page cost in 2026?

A basic landing page, single product, one call to action, mobile-responsive, costs $10–$15/month using Framer or Webflow with a template, or $500–$800 from a budget-tier Upwork freelancer. Neither price includes copywriting, which runs $300–$800 separately if you hire a conversion writer for it.

### Is it worth hiring a freelancer for a landing page?

Yes, when you need one page and your time is worth more than $50/hour. A 10-hour DIY build at $50/hour is $500 in real cost, which puts most competent freelancers in the same range once you add the tool fee. Freelancers make the most sense when you're building once, not repeatedly.

### What does an agency landing page include that a freelancer doesn't?

Agencies typically bundle strategy, copy, design, development, and QA in a single contract. Most freelancers cover one or two of those disciplines, so you coordinate the rest yourself. The agency advantage is one accountable team across the whole process; the tradeoff is cost ($2,000+) and lead time.

### Does a more expensive landing page convert better?

Not automatically. Unbounce's [2024 Conversion Benchmark Report](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/), which analyzed 41,000 landing pages, found that copy clarity matters more than design quality: pages written at a 5th–7th grade reading level convert at 11.1%, compared to 5.3% for professional-level writing. A $10/month Framer template with plain, direct language can outperform a $5,000 agency page that buries the offer in brand messaging.
